    Event log

      The project in brief

      A PLC runs the filler, capper and conveyor, but nothing checks the caps. Some bottles leave the capper with no cap or a loose, tilted one, which caused 23 customer complaints last month.

      This design adds one inspection point right after the capper. An IR reflective sensor reads each bottle neck, an ESP32 sorts the bottle as Good, Loose or Missing, and a gate 0.45 m downstream removes the bad ones.

      The sample data splits into four groups that never overlap. The closest two are 275 counts apart, about seven times the ±40 count noise, so fixed thresholds are enough. The parts cost Rs. 1,465 against the Rs. 3,000 cap.

      How it works, step by step

      1. A bottle leaves the capper and rides the belt (0.3 m/s, one bottle every 1.5 s) to the inspection station.
      2. For the 0.4 s it passes under the IR sensor, the ESP32 reads the ADC about 100 times a second.
      3. It takes the median of those readings, which removes the ±40 count noise.
      4. The median falls in a band: Good above 2100, Loose 950 to 2100, Missing 240 to 950. Below 240 means no bottle.
      5. A Good bottle goes on. A Loose or Missing bottle joins the reject queue with a fire time of mid-pass plus 1.5 s.
      6. When the fire time arrives, the ESP32 switches the optocoupler and relay for about 0.3 s and the gate pushes that bottle into the bin.
      7. The OLED, LEDs and counters update, so maintenance can see which defect is most common.

      The simulator models the IR path, the queue, the gate and the faults. The ultrasonic cross-check is part of the hardware design and is not simulated.

      Components in detail

      ESP32 DevKitRs. 600
      Role
      The controller. Reads the sensors, classifies bottles, runs the reject queue and drives every output.
      Placement
      Inside the control box beside the belt.
      Key specs
      Dual-core 240 MHz, 3.3 V logic, 12-bit ADC (0 to 4095). Use ADC1 pins (GPIO32 to 39), because ADC2 clashes with Wi-Fi. Wi-Fi is not needed for the demo.
      IR reflective sensorRs. 60
      Role
      Measures how much IR light bounces off the cap. A good cap reflects a lot, a loose one less, a missing one little, and an empty slot almost none.
      Placement
      On the bracket over the belt, 1 to 2 cm above the neck, facing down, with a small shroud.
      Key specs
      3.3 to 5 V, range about 2 to 30 cm depending on the module. A potentiometer sets sensitivity.
      Check first
      You need the analog output (AO). Many obstacle modules only give on/off, which cannot give the counts used here. Power it from 3.3 V so the analog output stays within the ESP32 limit.
      Ultrasonic HC-SR04Rs. 100
      Role
      Cross-checks cap height and helps catch a faulty IR sensor.
      Placement
      On the same bracket, beside the IR sensor, about 5 cm above the cap, pointing down.
      Key specs
      5 V, about 15 mA, range 2 to 400 cm, accuracy about 3 mm, 40 kHz, beam about 15°, 10 µs trigger pulse.
      Check first
      The echo pin outputs 5 V. A resistor divider fixes that, but resistors are not on the price sheet. Ask the organisers, or use a 3.3 V-tolerant HC-SR04 variant.
      Optocoupler moduleRs. 60
      Role
      Passes the signal by light so the ESP32 is electrically isolated from the relay side.
      Placement
      In the control box, between the ESP32 and the relay.
      Key specs
      The input LED side works from a 3.3 V logic signal. The output is a transistor that drives the relay input.
      1-channel relay moduleRs. 80
      Role
      The switch behind the reject gate. It closes for about 0.3 s when a bad bottle reaches the gate.
      Placement
      In the control box, close to the gate side.
      Key specs
      5 V coil, contacts commonly rated 10 A at 250 V AC. Many modules are active-low, so a LOW input turns it on.
      0.96 inch OLED displayRs. 250
      Role
      Shows the current verdict, ADC reading, queue length and defect counts.
      Placement
      Operator panel at eye level.
      Key specs
      SSD1306, 128 by 64 pixels, I2C (address usually 0x3C), 3.3 V.
      Active buzzerRs. 20
      Role
      Sounds the alarm when the sensor fails.
      Placement
      On the operator panel.
      Key specs
      3 to 5 V with a built-in oscillator, so a HIGH signal gives a fixed tone. Check the current it draws against the pin limit.
      LEDs (3)Rs. 15
      Role
      Green shows a pass, red shows a reject, yellow shows a sensor fault.
      Placement
      On the operator panel.
      Key specs
      About 2 V and 20 mA each, with a series resistor of about 220 Ω. Resistors are not on the price sheet, so ask the organisers or use LED modules that include one.
      Push buttons (3)Rs. 30
      Role
      Demo inputs: button 1 plays the next sample bottle, button 2 freezes the sensor reading, button 3 resets.
      Placement
      On the operator panel.
      Key specs
      Momentary switches wired to ground, read with the ESP32 internal pull-up, so no resistor is needed.
      Breadboard and jumper setRs. 100
      Role
      Solderless wiring for the prototype.
      Placement
      Inside the control box.
      Tip
      Keep the 5 V relay supply and the 3.3 V logic on separate rails, with a shared ground.
      Power adapter / USB supplyRs. 150
      Role
      Powers the ESP32, relay and sensors.
      Placement
      Plugged in outside the control box.
      Key specs
      5 V, ideally 1 to 2 A. The relay coil and the ESP32 are the main loads.

      The bottle width of 0.12 m follows from the 0.4 s pass time at 0.3 m/s. Each bad bottle gets its own fire time, so two in a row are both rejected.

      When the sensor fails

      FaultHow it is detectedResponse
      Stuck reading30 samples in a row with the same valueAlarm, yellow LED, reject every bottle
      Open circuitReading at or near 0 for 10 samplesSame
      Short circuitReading at or near 4095 for 10 samplesSame
      Recovery30 normal, changing readingsAlarm clears, inspection resumes

      While the sensor is faulty, bottle presence comes from the backup (the ultrasonic sensor or belt timing), and every bottle is rejected. Losing good bottles is safer than shipping bad ones.
